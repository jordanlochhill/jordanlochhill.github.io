Research profile

I am a PhD candidate in Artificial Intelligence at the University of Western Australia asking whether AI systems could share our kairotic positionality, the position within an unfolding situation from which a fitting moment becomes available, and what that would mean for alignment, morality and our own nature. I pursue it through philosophical inquiry and empirical exploration, building the systems I study: I am developing Kairos, a novel neural architecture in which an incoming signal, its learned representations and a language model advance at different rates while remaining responsive to one another. Its alignment premise is that systems sharing our temporal organisation would be less alien to us, and therefore more predictable. The project’s linguistic strand, accepted for presentation at ALS 2026, separates reduced latency from well-timed response through prosody, pauses, turn-taking and repair. Supervision across philosophy, computer science and linguistics, with earlier training in Ancient Greek, classical philosophy and medieval studies, lets me work from primary sources and running code alike. I build and run my own research infrastructure, from gradient-checked reference implementations to an agent-native experiment system on two GPU clusters.

Research

Kairos: a novel neural architecture for signal processing | 2026–present

Doctoral research and Prosody Labs; unpublished.

  • Architecture. Learned encoders and decoders let a sequence-model core (Gated DeltaNet, attention or a hybrid) run at its own rate, independent of the rates at which signals enter and leave the model, so one architecture serves raw audio, text and both together.
  • Raw-waveform audio. Encodes raw audio into lower-rate learned representations and reconstructs the waveform at approximately 23 dB signal-to-noise ratio on recorded audio in development experiments.
  • Full-duplex conversation. Extends Moshi (Défossez et al., 2024), the basis of NVIDIA’s PersonaPlex. Moshi hears and speaks through Mimi, a separately trained codec that quantises 24 kHz speech into eight discrete codebooks at a fixed 12.5 Hz; Kairos instead learns continuous representations from raw waveform end to end, on audio and text clocks that advance independently. Trains 0.8-billion-parameter models from Qwen3.5-0.8B on four NVIDIA H100s, against a Moshi-recipe replication at the same scale.
  • Byte-level language modelling from signals. Combines text encoded as a signal for Kairos with byte-level multi-token prediction, extending the 8-byte horizon of Gloeckle et al. (2024) to 32 bytes, scaling to 64. The 256-symbol byte vocabulary keeps many prediction heads cheap where token vocabularies make them a memory bottleneck, and the longer horizon trains a tokenizer-free model towards word-scale structure. Trained on four AMD MI210s and NVIDIA GPUs and evaluated on the CORE benchmark suite, alongside an original recurrent token mixer.
  • Evidence-led redesign. Earlier versions, tested inside recurrent and attention-based language models of 120–150 million parameters, redirected the architecture towards variable-rate processing.

Research infrastructure | Prosody Labs, 2026–present

  • Execute. An agent-native experiment execution system for Slurm: every run is a commit-pinned, auditable record queued through git, by hand or by AI coding agents, on UWA’s Kaya HPC cluster and a dedicated workstation, with guardrails for unattended use. Over 1,100 runs since July 2026.
  • Verified training. A gradient-checked reference implementation and a PyTorch training system validated against it keep large-scale results traceable to verified mathematics.
  • Distributed training. FSDP and DDP training on NVIDIA H100 and AMD MI210 GPUs, including a 2.26× faster training update in a matched four-H100 benchmark with loss and gradient agreement checks.
  • Research platform. A self-hosted Kubernetes (k3s) platform with TLS and single sign-on serves live training playgrounds, run review and analysis reports regenerated from committed data.
